# The autonomous company, two years out

By late 2028 the interesting question about a small company will not be which AI tools it uses. It will be which decisions it still routes to a person, and why.

Three things will be true.

**The org chart becomes a ledger.** Today's agents fail at the seams: sales does not know delivery is full, finance does not know support promised a credit. The companies that work will not have smarter agents. They will have one shared record that every role reads and writes, with every change logged. Coordination becomes a property of the data, not of meetings.

**Authority, not capability, is the design problem.** Models can already draft the discount, the reminder and the write-off. What they lack is a mandate. Winning firms will give each agent an explicit charter: what it may decide alone, what it must escalate, and what happens if nobody answers. Autonomy will be granted in currency amounts and widened on evidence, the way a junior employee earns trust.

**Failure handling is the product.** An agent that is right 95% of the time is unusable unless the company survives the other 5%. Heartbeats, durable mailboxes, peers that cover for a silent role, and replayable history will matter more than benchmark scores.

Humans do not disappear. They move to the edges: setting authority, taking the exceptions, owning relationships and regulators. In the Gulf, where trust is personal and compliance is local, that edge stays wide. A five-person firm will run the operations of fifty, and the five will spend their time on judgement.

The autonomous company is not a company without people. It is a company where every routine decision has an accountable owner that never sleeps, and every unusual one reaches a person with the context already attached.
